01Approach

Greybox is not grey. It is light, shape and framing.

A blockout is the first time a space exists. If it does not read with flat materials, it will not read with final art either. I treat every blockout as a level art pass in its simplest form: three tools, used on purpose.

Light

Light is my first navigation tool. One bright pool in a dark room says "go here" faster than any sign. I place key lights early and keep most of the frame dark, so the player reads the path and the threat from value alone. Colour is a signal: red means danger or a system, green means exit.

Shape

Silhouettes come before detail. I give each area one dominant shape: the vertical wall of the Dam, the repeating trusses of the turbine hall, the round cage of a stair tower. Scale contrast between the player and the structure gives the space weight, and a clear landmark gives orientation.

Composition

I design the frame the player sees at key moments. Door frames and windows become picture frames. Railings, tile grids and pillars become leading lines. Fog layers the depth into foreground, middle ground and background, so the eye goes to the next objective first.

05About

Readable spaces, with a little uncertainty.

Hi, I'm Maciej. I am a level designer from Wrocław, and I turn imaginary places into spaces a player can read.

I started in mobile puzzle games, where every level must teach something in a few seconds. That made me strict about readability. Now I work in 3D, and the question is the same: what does the player see first, and what does that tell them? In a blockout, light and shape answer that question.

I like spaces with a little intrigue: a light at the end of a corridor, a door that is open when it should not be, a structure too big to understand at first sight. I studied Creative Media and Game Design at the University of Lower Silesia and TouchDesigner at the Polish-Japanese Academy of Information Technology. Cinema and arcade history still shape how I frame a scene.

I want to grow on teams that care about the space as much as the mechanics, in level design or level art.
