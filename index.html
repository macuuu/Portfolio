01Approach

Greybox breathes through light, shape and composition.

A blockout is the first time a space exists. If it does not read with flat materials, it will not read with final art either. I treat every blockout as a level art pass in its simplest form.

Light

Light is my first navigation tool. One bright pool in a dark room says "go here" faster than any sign. I place key lights early and keep most of the frame dark, so the player reads the path and the threat from value alone.

Shape

Silhouettes come before detail. I give each area one dominant shape: the vertical wall of the Dam, the repeating trusses of the turbine hall, the round cage of a stair tower. Scale contrast between the player and the structure gives the space weight, and a clear landmark gives orientation.

Composition

I design the frame the player sees at key moments. Door frames and windows become picture frames. Railings, tile grids and pillars become leading lines. Fog layers the depth into foreground, middle ground and background, so the eye goes to the next objective first.

05About

Readable spaces, with a little uncertainty.

Hi, I'm Maciej, a Senior Level Designer with 7+ years of experience in puzzle and gameplay design.

I work primarily in Unreal Engine and Unity. Over my career, I've created thousands of match-3 levels that experimented with the player's ability to learn through play. In my work, I always aim to catch glimpses of intrigue and uncertainty in the player's decision-making.

My craft is shaped by, and shapes, industry-standard gameplay and design practices. I'm looking to grow as a game developer on projects that help shape the future of games.
